How to Remove Hard Water Spots from Windows

Hard water spots are mineral deposits, usually from sprinklers, and they need an acid-based cleaner to dissolve them. Regular glass cleaner won't work.

  1. Mix equal parts white vinegar and warm water in a spray bottle.
  2. Spray the spotted area and let it sit for 5–10 minutes so the vinegar can break down the minerals.
  3. Gently wipe with a soft microfiber cloth or non-scratch sponge.
  4. Rinse with clean water and dry with a clean microfiber cloth.
  5. Repeat on stubborn spots. Never use steel wool, scouring pads, or razor blades, which can scratch the glass or damage window tint.

When to call a pro: if spots are widespread, on second-story windows, or don't come off after a few tries. Hard water spot removal is included free with our window cleaning.

How to Prevent Sprinkler Stains on Windows

The best way to prevent sprinkler stains is to stop the water from reaching the glass.

  1. Walk around the house while your sprinklers run and note which heads hit the windows.
  2. Adjust the spray direction or arc so water lands on the lawn, not the house.
  3. Lower the pressure or swap in lower-angle nozzles near the house.
  4. Water early in the morning so less water sits and dries on the glass in the sun.
  5. Clean windows regularly so minerals don't build up and etch the glass.

When to call a pro: if your windows already have heavy spotting. Removal is included free with our cleaning.

How Often Should You Clean Your Windows in North Texas?

Most North Texas homes need professional window cleaning at least once a year, and twice a year is ideal.

  1. Spring: remove pollen, dust, and winter grime.
  2. Fall: get your home ready for the holidays and guests.
  3. Extra cleanings: if your home faces heavy sprinkler spray, construction dust, or you're selling your home.
  4. Between cleanings: wipe fingerprints and smudges on the inside as needed.

How to Prepare Your Home for a Window Cleaning

A few minutes of prep helps your window cleaning go quickly and smoothly.

  1. Unlock any gates so we can reach every window.
  2. Clear small items and breakables off window sills.
  3. Keep pets inside or secured during outdoor cleaning.
  4. For inside-and-out cleaning, make sure someone is home to let us in.
  5. Point out any windows that need extra attention, like hard water spots.

How to Count Your Windows for a Quote

Counting your windows correctly lets you know your price before you call.

  1. Walk around your home and count each window.
  2. Count double-hung, transom, arched, casement, and glass door windows as 1 each.
  3. Count sliding windows, door sidelights, and sliding glass doors as 2 each.

How to Get Streak-Free Windows

Streaks usually come from too much cleaner, dirty cloths, or cleaning in direct sun.

  1. Clean on a cloudy day or when the windows are in shade, so cleaner doesn't dry too fast.
  2. Wipe off loose dust and cobwebs first.
  3. Use a small amount of cleaner and a clean microfiber cloth or squeegee.
  4. If using a squeegee, pull it in one direction and wipe the blade after every stroke.
  5. Finish with a dry, lint-free microfiber cloth on the edges.

How to Clean Window Screens

Clean screens keep dirt from ending up right back on your glass.

  1. Carefully remove the screens and note which window each came from.
  2. Lay them flat on a clean surface outside.
  3. Brush off loose dirt with a soft brush.
  4. Gently wash with warm, soapy water and a soft brush or sponge.
  5. Rinse with a gentle spray of water (not a pressure washer, which can tear the mesh).
  6. Let them dry completely before putting them back.

When to call a pro: screen cleaning is included free with every window cleaning we do.

How to Clean Window Tracks

Window tracks collect dirt, dead bugs, and grime that can make windows hard to open.

  1. Vacuum loose dirt using a narrow crevice attachment.
  2. Spray the track with warm, soapy water or a vinegar-water mix and let it sit a few minutes.
  3. Scrub with an old toothbrush or small detail brush.
  4. Wipe clean with a cloth or paper towel wrapped around a flat tool.
  5. Dry the track fully.

When to call a pro: we offer track cleaning as an add-on for $3 per window.

How to Tell if a Foggy Window Needs Cleaning or a New Seal

If the fog is on the inside or outside surface, it can be cleaned. If it's between the two panes, the seal has failed.

  1. Wipe the inside surface. If the fog disappears, it was condensation or dirt.
  2. Wipe the outside surface the same way.
  3. If the fog is still there, it's trapped between the panes.
  4. Trapped fog means the window's seal has failed and cleaning can't fix it. Contact a window repair or replacement company.

How to Clean High and Second-Story Windows Safely

The safest way to clean high windows is from the ground with an extension pole, not by climbing a ladder.

  1. Avoid leaning ladders against windows or gutters.
  2. Use an extension pole with a scrubber and squeegee attachment.
  3. Work from the ground on stable, level footing.
  4. Never clean high windows in wind or rain.

When to call a pro: we clean second-story windows safely with extension poles at no extra charge.

How to Keep Your Windows Clean Longer

A few habits can keep your windows looking clean between professional cleanings.

  1. Adjust sprinklers so they don't hit the glass.
  2. Keep screens clean so dirt doesn't transfer to the glass.
  3. Trim plants and trees away from windows.
  4. Wipe away fingerprints and smudges on the inside as they appear.
  5. Schedule professional cleaning once or twice a year.

How to Get Your Windows Ready for the Holidays

Clean windows make your home brighter for holiday guests, parties, and photos.

  1. Schedule your window cleaning before decorations and lights go up.
  2. Book early, since October through December is the busiest season.
  3. Clean window sills and tracks so everything looks finished.
  4. Put up decorations after the glass is clean and dry.
